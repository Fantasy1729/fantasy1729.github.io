Soundtrack to Survival

How Gen Z Uses Music to Cope With Mental Health

Introduction: Music as Modern Medicine

For Gen Z, music is more than background noise—it's therapy, a coping mechanism, and an emotional lifeline. Whether it's lo-fi beats, mood-based playlists, or sad rap, the sounds we turn to are part of how we survive stress, anxiety, and emotional overload.

Lo-fi Beats: The Sound of Stillness

Lo-fi music blends mellow rhythms, jazz chords, and gentle beats that help listeners focus, relax, or sleep. It's minimalist, non-intrusive, and emotionally neutral—perfect for journaling, studying, or decompressing after a hard day.

"I usually listen to lofi beats when I study. It allows me to clear my head and focus on whatever work I have to do."

Mood-Based Playlists: Emotional Curation

Playlists titled "songs to cry to," "late night drive," or "anxiety relief" have become digital toolkits for Gen Z. These aren't about genre—they're about feeling. They're how we sort and survive emotions.

"Sometimes I don't even know what I'm feeling until I hear it in a song."

Sad Rap: When Music Hits Too Close

Artists like JuiceWRLD, XXXTentacion, Lil Peep, and NF turn pain into poetry. Their lyrics hit hard because they’re honest. They talk about anxiety, heartbreak, addiction—and they don’t hold back.

"You saved millions, but no one saved you."

With minimalist beats, distorted vocals, and emotional depth, sad rap feels like reading someone’s diary. And when the artists themselves pass away young, their music becomes a memorial.

What Students Say

From dorm rooms to late-night walks, Duke students we interviewed told us how music gives them space to breathe, reflect, and reset. Whether through lo-fi or a playlist called “overthinking at 2AM,” music has become a form of personal care.

Conclusion: Music is Emotional Infrastructure

For Gen Z, music isn’t just about fun. It’s how we hold ourselves together. Whether we’re crying, calming down, or just trying to understand what we’re feeling, music meets us where we are—and helps us move forward.
